The academic year is split into two semesters. The winter semester starts in the second half of September and lasts 19 weeks: for 13 weeks there's teaching according to the timetable, and weeks 14–19 are the exam period, when your knowledge is tested in the courses that end with an exam (marked ZK in the Electronic index). The exam period is usually the toughest part of the semester, but some people are done by week 15, with ticks in their index, and can go on holiday, start a summer job or do whatever they like.

In your first semester your timetable will be assigned to you. It should be published in the information system at [vutbr.cz/studis](https://www.vutbr.cz/studis) around mid-September. Check the Individual timetable (Individuální rozvrh) tab. From then on you'll be able to influence your timetables yourself.

The colours in the timetable show the type of class:

- green – lectures (P),
- blue – exercises (Cz),
- orange – labs (L), online (O),
- yellow – everything else, most often computer exercises (Cp).

Lectures usually take place in large lecture halls for 150 or more people. A course is often lectured by several teachers, and you can choose whose lectures you go to. In the information system, click Student -> Timetables -> Course timetable and type the course abbreviation into the search field; the system will show all lectures/exercises/labs of the course, so you can easily choose the lecturer you prefer. Lectures are optional, but we recommend attending them.

![A course in the timetable: BPC-MA2 (O), T8/T 5.03, Kolářová Edita, doc. RNDr., Ph.D.](/images/rozvrh.webp){width="300"}

There are two kinds of exercises. Either computer exercises ("Cp"), where you apply theory on a computer, or fundamentals exercises ("Cz"), where you mostly work through problems. This is a more personal form of teaching: you'll be in a group of about 20 classmates. Exercises are mandatory, and you can't choose which ones you attend.

The last form of teaching is the mandatory labs, marked "L" in the timetable. Here you put your knowledge into practice, most often by taking measurements. You work in groups of 2–4 people, and the output of your lab work is a lab report. Often you'll also need a written preparation for the particular task, or your readiness to measure will be checked in another way, for example with a short test.

The second line of a course in the timetable (see the image above) shows the room and building where the class takes place. FEEC has 3 buildings: Technická 8 and 10, which are joined into one building, and Technická 12, which is harder to find your way around at first. You may also see a week marker for classes that don't take place every week (S is even, L is odd). This refers to the week of the year; the first week of teaching is week 38, so it's even.

Next, let's look at the Electronic index, which you'll find at [vutbr.cz/studis](https://www.vutbr.cz/studis). What interests us there is the type of each course and how it's completed.

![Electronic index: abbreviation, course name, language, type, credits, VSP and completion, e.g. BMA1 Mathematics 1, cs, P, 7, Yes, zá,zk](/images/index.webp){width="420"}

P means a compulsory course, which, unlike all the others, follows a special rule. If, for example, you don't pass Maths 1 in your first semester, you'll have to enrol in it (or rather, it will enrol itself) in the following academic year, i.e. in your 3rd semester. If you don't pass it the second time either, your studies will be terminated.

A course can be completed by:

- **Credit (zá)** – for simpler courses, where e.g. attending classes is enough to complete them.
- **Graded credit (kl).** Unlike a plain credit, you also get a points score, so you can expect a test.
- **Credit and exam (zá,zk).** Most courses are completed this way. It means meeting the credit requirements (e.g. handing in lab reports, getting a certain number of points from tests during the semester, handing in a project…) and then also passing the final exam.
- **Exam (zk).** You don't need to meet any credit requirements, and you'll still be admitted to the exam.

FEEC, like most other universities, uses the ECTS grading scale from A to F. Throughout the semester you can earn points from various activities (tests, lab reports, activity in exercises…). These are added to your exam score, if the course has an exam, and the result can range from 0 to 100 points. If you get 90 or more, you get an A, which corresponds to the numerical grade 1; 80–89 points means a B and the numerical grade 1.5; 70–79 is a C and the grade 2, and so on down to the last grade, F, for 0–49 points, which means the failing numerical grade 4.

You'll find the full text of the regulations at [fekt.vut.cz/pro_studenty/studijni_predpisy](https://www.fekt.vut.cz/pro_studenty/studijni_predpisy) under the name "Study and Examination Regulations of Brno University of Technology" (Studijní a zkušební řád VUT v Brně).

## What to watch out for: a few tips

Watch out for the physics exercises! They follow their own separate notice, which is published at the start of the semester in eLearning. People often forget about it. Likewise, it's a good idea to read the notices for all your other courses at the start of the semester.

Every student is entitled to one regular exam attempt, one resit, and a limited number of second resits. For bachelor's students the limit is 6, for master's students 4. So it pays to be well prepared for the first attempt.

- For oral exams it's a good idea to dress smartly, e.g. a blazer. For written exams this usually isn't expected.
- You need at least 30 credits every academic year, otherwise your studies will be terminated. Careful: in your first semester you need at least 11.
- Over your whole bachelor's degree you need 180 credits and to meet the requirements you'll find under Check of completed courses (Kontrola absolvovaných předmětů) in the information system.
- If you fail a compulsory course, you must enrol in it again and pass it the following academic year, otherwise your studies will be terminated.
- Also keep in mind that once you enrol in a compulsory elective course, it becomes compulsory for you.
- If your studies are terminated, you can apply again. Usually all courses you completed with a D or better will be recognised, but you need to submit a request for recognition.
- Studying at public universities is free for the standard length of study + 1 year. That means a bachelor's programme can be studied free of charge for 4 years (under the Higher Education Act, effective from 1 April 2021). All your studies count towards this time, including those at other universities.
- A good motivation for good grades is the merit scholarship, which can be up to 5,000 CZK a month. You need an average of 1–1.5; from your second year it can be awarded based on your average from the previous year and paid monthly to your account.
- You can easily import your timetable into Google Calendar using an \*.ics file, and from there to your phone.
- If you miss a mandatory class, email the teacher in time to apologise and agree on how to make up for it. You also have to upload your excuse to the information system.
