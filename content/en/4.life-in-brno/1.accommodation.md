Do you live far from Brno? Then finding accommodation will definitely be one of your most important tasks. Fortunately, the BUT dormitories have enough capacity, and usually 2–5% of the total capacity is still free after all students have been accommodated. If you prefer privacy and don't want to live in the dorms with lots of classmates, Brno has a wide range of flats and private student residences for rent. Renting a room in a flat usually costs around 4,000–10,000 CZK per person, depending on the size of the flat and the location, plus utilities (sometimes included in the rent). There are plenty of offers: just look on Facebook or Google.

## Off to the KaM dorms…

If you decide to live in the dorms, BUT's Accommodation and Catering Service (Koleje a menzy, KaM) has 4 complexes with a total capacity of over 6,000 beds. For FEEC, the most relevant are probably the ones closest to the faculty: the Pod Palackého vrchem dormitories (PPV, blocks Axx), the Purkyňovy dormitories (blocks Bxx) and possibly the Mánesovy (Dxx) or Listovy (Cxx) dormitories. Those are closer to the city centre, and who'd want to commute when they could be at the faculty in a five-minute walk, right? Each complex has several numbered blocks. You can take a virtual tour of all the complexes, rooms and catering facilities at [www.kam.vutbr.cz](https://www.kam.vutbr.cz). What's on offer differs by block, from single rooms with their own bathroom and kitchenette to triple rooms with a shared bathroom and kitchenette.

### Booking accommodation

If you're new to the dorms and haven't stayed with KaM BUT before, you'll need to log in to the IS (the university information system at [vut.cz](https://www.vut.cz)). Applying isn't difficult, just lengthy, but with the guide you'll manage.

You'll find a clear guide at [www.kam.vutbr.cz](https://www.kam.vutbr.cz), specifically the link "**Instructions for accommodation for the academic year 2026/27**" (Pokyny pro ubytování na akademický rok 2026/27) at the top of the website's front page.

Moving into the dorms works like this: you show your ID card or student card at the reception and sign for your key. The biggest advantage is the extremely fast dormitory network (kolejnet), which is only available in the dorms. You'll find out how to activate your connection at [kn.vutbr.cz](https://kn.vutbr.cz); internet costs around 150 CZK a month.

Another advantage is the catering facilities and canteens, which are the best of all the Brno universities. Closest to FEEC are Menza Kolejní, Menza Purkyňova, Restaurace Q and Pizzerie Mozzarella. A bit further away (near the Faculty of Civil Engineering) is Menza Rybkova, which even has a pastry shop. In all of them you can pay cashless with your student card, with the money deducted from your account, or if you have the credit system, your spending is added to your accommodation bill. You'll find out more about the credit system and how to activate it at [www.kam.vutbr.cz](https://www.kam.vutbr.cz).

### Tips and tricks for the dorms

Read the contract you're signing; it will answer many of your questions. It's important to sign the contract on time: missing the deadline is the most common reason students don't get into the dorms and lose their reserved place.

You can borrow a vacuum cleaner or cleaning products at the reception. You'll find all the dormitory services on the KaM website.

Read the rules for using the internet in the dorms. At [kn.vutbr.cz](https://kn.vutbr.cz) you'll find a guide to registering your devices on the dormitory network and everything else to do with the internet.

**CAREFUL:** Don't download torrents; you risk a month-long ban.\
Watch out for rooms X41 (841, 541 ...): they're triple rooms, i.e. the living area is in one room and the kitchenette in the other, and the rooms have a balcony.

Erasmus students will be accommodated in block A03 at Palačák (the Pod Palackého vrchem dormitories). If you prefer peace and quiet, choose another block. But if you're into non-stop partying, this is the block for you.

You have to bring your own kitchen equipment: pots, plates, cutlery. You'll find a duvet and pillow in your room.

### Dormitory Messenger and Discord groups

You can connect with other dormitory residents not only in person but also online: almost all dormitories have a Messenger group, and you'll also find dedicated channels on the faculty Discords. We have dedicated channels for all the relevant dormitories (Purkyňovy, Listovy, Mánesovy, PPV).

### What the dorms look like

What's on offer differs by block. The Pod Palackého vrchem dormitories are the biggest, mostly with double rooms with their own bathroom and toilet. At the Purkyňovy and Listovy dormitories you'll find triple and double rooms with a shared kitchenette and bathroom on each floor.

## Renting a flat

Brno is a student city, so, mainly thanks to Masaryk University and its complete lack of dormitories, the flat rental market works very well. There are lots of flats on offer, but the problem is the price. You'll hardly find better accommodation for 2–4 people at a better price than the dorms (the Listovy and Purkyňovy dormitories cost roughly 3,700–4,000 CZK per person a month, the Palačák dormitories around 4,800 CZK + 150 CZK for internet). Flat prices are currently very unpleasant: for an average one-bedroom flat (1+1) you'll pay around 14,000 CZK including utilities. Sharing is the only way to get a more pleasant price.

There are, however, many things to watch out for:

**Summer holidays** at university last about 4 months, depending on how you schedule your exams in the exam period. If you don't plan to stay in Brno, the dorms are simple: you just move out and don't pay anything. With a flat it's not so simple: you're usually bound by contract to rent all year round, which can end up more expensive than the dorms. Many landlords will agree to reduced rent over the holidays, but you need to think about it in advance.

**Choosing a flat**: Lots of offers + lots of demand = a problem. It's very unlikely that you and your friends will get the first flat you like. Usually lots of other people are interested in the same flat or room, and it's pure chance whether the owners choose you.

Often you won't rent a whole flat, but just a place for yourself: flat SHARING. In that case someone has rented the flat, students live in it, and when one of them leaves, they look for a replacement. So you find a flat, but you'll share it with another student, just like in the dorms (perhaps with the advantage over the dorms that you meet them before you move in). If you want a room to yourself, it's somewhat more expensive.

**The price** is a very tricky item. It's common to state the rent but not the cost of utilities (water, electricity, heating, internet…). That's usually the case when the price looks too good. In any case, always ask in advance whether the price includes utilities and services, and how much the monthly rent comes to once they're included.

### Where to find a flat

A good website for finding flats is [ulovdomov.cz](https://www.ulovdomov.cz). You can choose whether you're looking to rent a whole flat or a shared one. Search for a location, and available flats will appear on the map. You'll also find lots of guides and good advice about renting flats there, which you should definitely read in advance.

Facebook is also handy, specifically the group "Bydlení v Brně, Bydlení Brno" (Housing in Brno). Just follow it; new options appear every day.

### How to choose a flat

You definitely need to go to a viewing. You can show the landlord you're a nice person, and at the same time see the flat and find out whether anything's missing or bothers you. Definitely don't take the first flat you like without seeing it. Nothing's worse than living for a whole year somewhere unpleasant and badly connected.

### Advice on renting a flat

In your first year we strongly recommend the dorms. If you don't know Brno yet and can't come to Brno every week for viewings, you can't go wrong with the dorms. In the dorms you're almost certain to get a room. The standard of living is fine and, above all, you'll be surrounded by lots of people willing to help in those first hectic months at university. If you don't like the dorms, it's no problem to move to a flat after a semester. After the first semester many people don't manage their studies and have to leave Brno, freeing up places in flats.

And the best part: from the dorms it's really close to the faculty. When you see your first-year timetable (around mid-September), you'll appreciate it.
