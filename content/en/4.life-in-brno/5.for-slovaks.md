Hi, fellow countrymen. I'm really glad you've decided to join us, the Slovaks in Brno. Brno is a beautiful student city full of young people, pubs and similar nice things. I hope the following lines will help you at the start so you don't get lost.

## Travel

Even as a student abroad, you can get a discount or travel for free in Slovakia, if the "quota" isn't used up. You'll need a ton of patience and a confirmation of study, which you'll get from your study officer at the Study Office. They won't confirm the ZSSK (Slovak Railways) form for you at the Study Office. At some ZSSK offices it's enough to send them the electronic confirmation of study, which you can download in Studis.

In the Czech Republic, the universal student card (ISIC) gives you a discount of around 50% on trains and buses, but only within the Czech Republic. You can travel with the discount up to the border point with Slovakia and split your journey to save money. For example, Brno – Bratislava: in Brno you buy a ticket to Kúty with the ISIC discount, and from Kúty to Bratislava another ticket with your ZSSK student card, while staying on the same international train.

In my opinion, it's also worth buying a "šalinkarta", a public transport season ticket, through BrnoID. It works out much better than constantly buying a ticket, or forgetting to buy one. You'll find out more in the [Transit passes](/en/life-in-brno/transit-passes) section.

## Banks

An account in the Czech Republic isn't just worth having; it's actually essential for paying for accommodation. And everything is easier with one. The dorms will want a direct debit from you. I recommend opening an account at the Czech "sister" bank of your Slovak one. For example, Slovenská sporiteľňa and Česká sporiteľňa don't charge each other any fees for transferring money, not even for sending it to another country. Bring another confirmation of study there so that you don't have to pay for running the account.

## Phones

Unfortunately, Slovak operators don't accept the Czech ISIC, like many other institutions and companies. So you probably won't officially get any student plans. But if you find a really good plan, you don't need to bother with a second operator in the Czech Republic. There are three mobile operators in the Czech Republic: O2, T-Mobile and Vodafone. Don't be fooled by the names; they have very little in common with their Slovak "colleagues". If you dig deeper, you'll find different tariffs for Slovakia and other differences. After roaming charges were abolished, life was great for a while, but the operators found a lever for this too: Orange, for example, raises prices if you call, text or use more data abroad than at home.

## Insurance

As a Slovak citizen, you're insured in Slovakia and the state pays your health insurance. So you should see doctors in Slovakia. But illness doesn't choose, and sooner or later you'll have to see a doctor in Brno.

### What then?

You certainly have a European Health Insurance Card from your health insurance company, which should be valid all over Europe. Some doctors in Brno accept it and will treat you without any problems. Others may send you back to Slovakia, or you'll have to pay for the treatment and then ask your insurance company for a refund. This process can be simplified with a document from a Czech insurance company (e.g. VZP), which lets the insurance companies settle the transactions directly between themselves, so you don't have to carry invoices and receipts to Slovakia. However, this document is only valid in necessary cases. If the doctor tells you that's not your case, you're out of luck. To get this certificate (a so-called "pendler"), you need a confirmation of study. At a branch of the General Health Insurance Company of the Czech Republic (VZP), they'll issue it on the spot, valid for as long as your confirmation of study. If you don't have it with you, they'll issue it for three months. In hospitals the doctors are mostly nice and helpful, and many of them are Slovak, so don't be afraid to go to the emergency department of the big hospitals, where they'll be happy to take care of you.

## Other things

At the faculty, don't be afraid to speak and write in Slovak: everyone will understand you, and some of the teachers are even Slovak. As a foreigner, you should register with the Foreign Police. If you live in the dorms, they'll give you a card for the Foreign Police to fill in when you collect your key. Otherwise you should go to the Foreign Police yourself and register.

International students without permanent residence in the Czech Republic must pay the local municipal waste fee every year. The fee is paid directly to the City of Brno and isn't included in the rent for the dorms or a private flat. We recommend paying it through the BrnoID platform. You'll find more information at: [www.brno.cz/w/mistni-poplatek-za-komunalni-odpad-v-brne](https://www.brno.cz/w/mistni-poplatek-za-komunalni-odpad-v-brne)

The Czech alternative to cp.sk is [idos.idnes.cz](https://idos.idnes.cz) or the DPMB app; more information in the [How does Brno work?](/en/life-in-brno/transport) section.

I hope it wasn't too much information and I haven't just confused you, but maybe this knowledge will come in handy one day.

So welcome, and good luck at the perFEKT faculty, better than any in Slovakia.
