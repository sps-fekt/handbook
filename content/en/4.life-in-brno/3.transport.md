Brno is about to become your new home. And feeling lost at home is no good. To make the beginning easier, here's at least some of the most basic information. But you'll have to explore Brno yourself, or with us through PerFEKT assistance.

## How public transport works

In Brno you can travel by four kinds of public transport: šalina (the local word for tram), trolleybus, bus and train. Below we'll try to describe simply how, where and by what you can get around Brno.

Current public transport timetables in Brno are at [dpmb.cz](https://www.dpmb.cz), where you can download line and stop timetables, or print a personal timetable at [www.idsjmk.cz](https://www.idsjmk.cz). To search for connections and buy single tickets within the South Moravian Region (IDS JMK), you can use the Poseidon mobile app. It's also useful to track the current position of vehicles in the DPMBinfo or iRIS app, or right in Google Maps.

You can easily walk between the Technická 10 and 12 campuses in under 3 minutes. To get to the centre, take tram 12, which starts from the Technologický park stop. The ride from Technologický park to the main railway station (Hlavní nádraží) takes about 25 minutes, depending on traffic.

Using the tram 12 route, we'll show you where you can change to get, for example, food, a transit pass, to a party or to the city centre.

Tram 12 goes from Technologický park via these stops (not a complete list, only the main ones):

**Červinkova** – here you'll find a canteen, the Purkyňovy dormitories, Roj kebab (but the one at Skácelova is better) or, for example, the pub-café The Roses Beer & Coffee.

**Skácelova** – here you'll find Roj kebab (recommended) and pubs, and you can change to bus 67 towards Avion Shopping Park. Take it either to the Královo Pole shopping centre, where there's a Tesco and other shops with clothes, stationery, a newsagent, a cheese shop and more, or ride to the terminus and, after more than half an hour, reach Avion Shopping Park, where besides IKEA you'll find everything you can think of.

You can also change to bus 72 (towards Technologický park), which takes you to the Technická, Podnikatelská and Kolejní stops, almost to the faculty's door. Among other stops, it runs from Skácelova and Husitská, where other lines meet.

**Klusáčkova** – here you'll find the Listovy dormitories, the Moravian Library and shops such as Billa, a print shop and others.

**Konečného náměstí** – also known as Konečňák. BTB students change here to trolleybus 25 on the way to Bohunice, to the Faculty of Medicine of Masaryk University, where a large part of their teaching takes place.

**Česká** – now you're really in the city centre. Česká, the name of the street, also called Čára ("the Line"), is where hundreds of people meet every day. Specifically, "Čára under the clock" (on the corner by Komerční banka) is the most common meeting point in the centre of Brno. If you follow the crowd, you'll reach Svoboďák, or Náměstí Svobody (Freedom Square), probably the most important square in Brno. Various food, wine and beer festivals and markets take place here several times a year. The square is dominated by the astronomical clock built in 2010, which looks less like a clock and more like a phallus, a rocket or a giant vibrator. Perhaps that's why it's become a very popular sight and the butt of many jokes. During important events on the square, the clock is often wrapped in a white cover that looks remarkably like a tampon. Well, you can find that in the streets of Brno too.

BPC-AUD students have their second school, the Janáček Academy of Performing Arts (JAMU), close to Česká; part of their teaching takes place there.

From the Česká stop you can walk to almost any corner of the city centre: Špilberk, Petrov, Náměstí Svobody, and all kinds of clubs, bars, discos, restaurants, cafés and tea rooms. Just wander through the streets and don't worry about getting lost. You won't, because wherever you come out, you can always reach the tram tracks, which lead either to Česká, from where you'll find your way, or to Moravské náměstí, which is almost within sight of Česká. Take a trip to the centre of Brno and don't worry, you'll surely come across lots of places that interest you.

**Hlavní nádraží** (main railway station) – here you can change to a train or walk to the Vaňkovka shopping centre, with clothes and electronics shops and a big Albert hypermarket.

**Zvonařka** – a large bus station, also close to the Vaňkovka shopping centre.

## Night buses

Night falls, trams and trolleybuses start returning to the depots, and there are fewer and fewer vehicles with the DPMB logo on the streets. Unlike other Czech cities, Brno has one great feature: night bus lines that all meet at regular intervals at a transport hub (the main railway station) and then all set off in every direction at once. If you need to get home from the city centre, just remember that night buses leave the main railway station from 23:00 to 05:00 every hour on the hour (and also at 23:30), and on nights before a non-working day (Friday and Saturday) also every half hour (00:30, 1:30, 4:30), except 2:30 and 3:30.

The Listovy and Purkyňovy dormitories are served by lines 93 and 99 (line 93 only goes to the Skácelova stop, from where you can walk to Purkyňky), the Pod Palackého vrchem dormitories only by line 99 (Kolejní stop), and the Mánesovy dormitories by lines 90 and 91 (Tylova stop).

Of course, night buses also run from the dorms into the city. If you plan to fall for the nightlife, you'll soon find your way around the night buses.
