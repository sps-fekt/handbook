If you haven't yet got lost in the sea of abbreviations as a newcomer, you certainly will at enrolment or in the first days of your studies. The faculty has several study programmes. They focus on electrical engineering and cybernetics (BPC-AMT, Automation and Measurement), electrical engineering with a narrower focus on designing electronic circuits and systems (BPC-MET, Microelectronics and Technology), electrical and power engineering (BPC-SEE, Power Electrical and Electronic Engineering), classic and mobile communication, smart grids and the Internet of Things (BPC-TLI, Telecommunication and Information Systems), IT systems security (BPC-IBE, Information Security), a wide range of areas in low-voltage electronics and communication technologies (BPC-EKT, Electronics and Communication Technologies), electrical engineering combined with medical sciences (BPC-BTB, Biomedical Technology and Bioinformatics), electrical engineering combined with English (BPC-APE, English in Electrical Engineering and Communication Technologies), and electrical engineering combined with music theory (BPC-AUD, Audio Engineering, specialising in sound production or technology). New programmes have also been created recently, BPC-EMU (E-mobility and Sustainability) and BPC-NCP (Chip Design and Modern Semiconductor Technologies), in response to the needs of industry and changing demands of society.

What does this mean for you? At first you'll be flooded with a huge amount of information, but not all of it applies to everyone. You need to filter out what's relevant to you using the programme abbreviations. Every notice, regulation and timetable states, by abbreviation, who it applies to. For example, biomedicine students get their card at Masaryk University, but this doesn't concern other programmes, because the notice says "applies to BPC-BTB".
